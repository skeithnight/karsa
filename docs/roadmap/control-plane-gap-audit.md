# Control Plane Gap Audit Report

This audit assesses the architectural dependency graph and priorities of the Virtual Investment Firm (VIF) control plane following the closure of Sprint-35. It challenges the roadmap placement of the Risk Engine relative to critical decision-making and learning loop engines (Decision Journal, CIO, and Post-Mortem).

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Based on a detailed analysis of VIF architectural dependencies, the current roadmap ordering (placing the Risk Engine before the Decision Journal and CIO Engine) is **invalid**. 

* **Is the Risk Engine really the highest priority remaining context?**
  No. While the Risk Engine is essential for ex-ante risk budget generation and limit calculations, it is an analytical service. 
* **Are the Decision Journal and CIO Engine more fundamental?**
  Yes. The Decision Journal and CIO Engine establish the core cryptographic trade authorization, decision audit ledger, and hindsight-prevention controls. Implementing Risk before securing the core decision loop results in building a high-fidelity risk metrics generator for an unauthenticated, unaudited trading loop.

Therefore, the roadmap must be reprioritized to implement the Decision Journal, CIO Engine, and Post-Mortem Engine before the Risk Engine and Thesis Engine evolution.

---

## 2. Control Plane Dependency Analysis

The VIF control plane dependencies follow this sequential path:
`Decision Journal → CIO → Execution → Portfolio → Performance → Review → Governance → Allocation`

Below is the status of each link in the dependency path:

1. **Decision Journal → CIO**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently missing`. The CIO Engine cannot authorize target allocations against strategy nodes without an immutable pre-outcome reasoning record from the Decision Journal.
2. **CIO → Execution**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently stubbed`. The Execution Engine's PEP signature check requires a cryptographic signature generated by the CIO decision ledger. It currently validates against test signatures.
3. **Execution → Portfolio**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: Realized (implemented). Real-time position/cash ledger balances update based on fills from Execution.
4. **Portfolio → Performance**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: Realized (implemented). Performance calculates returns based on holdings snapshots.
5. **Decision Journal → Performance**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently stubbed`. Calculating ex-post Brier scores (which evaluate confidence vs outcomes) requires the pre-outcome confidence levels stored in the Decision Journal. Performance tests currently pass confidence values as mocks.
6. **Decision Journal → Review**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently stubbed`. The Review Engine performs qualitative hindsight checks by comparing post-hoc summaries with pre-outcome rationales in the Decision Journal.
7. **Performance & Review → Post-Mortem**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently missing`. Post-Mortem requires performance scorecards and qualitative reviews to classify failures.
8. **Post-Mortem → Governance & Allocation**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently missing`. Post-Mortem feeds root-cause adjustments back to Governance (adjust limits) and Allocation (resize weights).
9. **Governance & Allocation → CIO**:
   - **Type**: `required`
   - **Status**: `currently missing` (Governance PDP checks are implemented, but target weights cannot be proposed to a non-existent CIO ledger).

## 3. Execution Authority Audit

We inspected the Execution Engine in [services.py](file:///Users/dwiki.nugraha/dwikicode/karsa/src/karsa/execution/application/services.py#L82-L106) and [test_execution.py](file:///Users/dwiki.nugraha/dwikicode/karsa/tests/karsa/execution/test_execution.py#L45-L53):

* **How `decision_id` is produced today**: The `decision_id` is passed directly as the `correlation_id` parameter to the Execution Engine from external clients, rather than being fetched from a real ledger.
* **Whether CIO authority is real or simulated**: Simulated. The `decision_auth_port` (`DecisionAuthorizationPort`) has no real production adapter class, only the `MockDecisionAuthorizationAdapter` used in testing.
* **Whether signatures originate from a real aggregate**: No. Signatures are simulated using a test key pair generated ad-hoc in test fixtures.
* **Whether execution is currently dependent on placeholders**: Yes. Since the CIO Engine and Decision Journal do not exist, the Execution Engine PEP validation operates entirely on mock/simulated decision parameters.

**Verdict**: `STUB_AUTHORITY`

---

## 4. Decision Journal Gap Analysis

We evaluated the Review Engine's operation in the absence of the Decision Journal:

* **Hindsight Prevention**: Broken. Without an immutable write-once pre-outcome record, there is no way to prevent agents from modifying their expectations after trade outcomes are known.
* **Forecast Calibration**: Mocked. Ex-post Brier score and confidence calibrations computed by [CalibrationService](file:///Users/dwiki.nugraha/dwikicode/karsa/src/karsa/performance/application/service.py#L35-L111) run on hardcoded test values rather than real pre-outcome logs.
* **Thesis Traceability**: Broken. The connection between research hypotheses, active parameters, and execution outcomes cannot be audited.

**Verdict**: `BLOCKING_GAP`

---

## 5. Post-Mortem Dependency Analysis

We evaluated whether the Post-Mortem Engine can be implemented before the Decision Journal and CIO:

* **Root Cause Attribution**: Cannot be implemented. Attributing failure causes requires comparing execution outcomes against the pre-trade [DecisionRationale](file:///Users/dwiki.nugraha/dwikicode/karsa/docs/architecture/18-decision-journal.md#L79) (Decision Journal) and target allocation authorizations (CIO).
* **Reasoning Replay**: Impossible. There is no pre-outcome reasoning state to replay.
* **Governance Feedback**: Broken. Adjusting policy bounds based on failure lessons requires a valid, traced decision-making history.

**Verdict**: `DEPENDENCY_EXISTS`

## 6. Risk Engine Priority Challenge

We challenged the assumption that the Risk Engine should be prioritized in Sprint-37:

* **Consumers needing Risk**: Capital Allocation (requires covariance forecasts) and Governance PDP (requires VaR limits). Currently, both contexts use simple mock values or historical estimates to function.
* **Consumers needing Decision Journal**: Performance (Brier scores), Review (qualitative analyses), Post-Mortem (failure reviews), and Execution (decision correlation).
* **Consumers needing CIO**: Execution (decision signature validation) and Capital Allocation (target weight approval).
* **Impact on VIF learning loop**: Building the Risk Engine first does not unblock the VIF learning loop because the loop remains broken at the trade authorization (CIO), pre-outcome auditing (Decision Journal), and failure feedback (Post-Mortem) stages. Building the control plane first completes the operational loop.

## 8. Roadmap Re-Prioritization

We evaluated two re-prioritization options:

* **Option A**: Performance → Risk → Thesis → Decision Journal → CIO → Post-Mortem
* **Option B**: Performance → Decision Journal → CIO → Post-Mortem → Risk → Thesis

**Selected Option**: **Option B**

* **Architectural Justification**: Option B satisfies VIF architecture principles by establishing a secured, audited control plane first. Resolving the signature validation and hindsight-prevention gap immediately provides real inputs to Performance, Review, and future Post-Mortem engines. The Risk Engine and Thesis Engine can then be evolved in an environment with real, cryptographically verifiable data feeds.

---

## 9. Architecture Delta Analysis

Comparing the current repository to the target VIF architecture:

* **Largest remaining architectural gap**: The lack of a unified cryptographic control plane (CIO authority and Decision Journal ledger) to sign and verify orders before they are sent to the Execution Engine.
* **Largest governance gap**: The lack of Decision Journal pre-outcome context snapshots to validate that active PDP limit checks match the actual reasoning state during execution.
* **Largest learning-loop gap**: The missing Post-Mortem Engine to feed failure lessons back into Governance, Thesis, and Research contexts, leaving the learning loop open.
* **Largest replayability gap**: The lack of Decision Journal and CIO ledgers preventing a complete historical replay of the system's reasoning state (we can replay fills and portfolio states, but we cannot replay *why* those trades were decided).

4. **Sprint-39**: Post-Mortem Engine Foundation (Implement failure classification taxonomy and feedback loops)
5. **Sprint-40**: Risk Engine Foundation (Implement ex-ante risk modeling, VaR, stress testing, covariance forecasting)
6. **Sprint-41**: Thesis Engine Evolution (Refactor legacy repositories, clean up ActiveThesis debt, finalize Postgres schemas)
7. **Sprint-42**: Research Engine Foundation (Implement signal sandbox and prompt template auditing)
8. **Sprint-43**: Regime Engine Foundation (Implement market regime classification)
9. **Sprint-44**: Knowledge Graph Foundation (Implement semantic query and storage layer)
